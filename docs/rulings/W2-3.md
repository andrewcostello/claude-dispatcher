## Ruling 1 — the policy: a clause's fate is its record's, sentence by sentence

**Decided.** The header `Reddens under a body on:` is retired from this file.
Each sentence takes the spelling its ledger record fixes:

* a `held` observation → `Measured under:` the mutation sentence, followed by
  the **claim id** in parentheses — `(ml-…)`, never the `mlo-` observation id
  (`citations` refuses that spelling) — and the revision it was last
  re-derived `held` at;
* a prediction → `Predicted (unmeasured) under:` the sentence verbatim,
  followed by `(mlp-…)`.
* a prediction whose record **does not exist yet** → the same label and
  sentence, followed by `(prediction record owed — docs/rulings/W2-3.md,
  ruling N)` in place of the id. This is an interim shape, not a third kind
  of record, and it is fail-open by construction: `citations` scans for ids,
  so a sentence with none is invisible to it — the same invisibility every
  unlabelled clause had before this ruling. Nothing mechanical verifies it;
  what verifies it is the **owed list** at the end of Ruling 3, which names
  each such sentence, the command that records it, the role that runs it,
  and the id the record will carry. The shape exists today for exactly two
  sentences (rows 31 and 38, Ruling 3) and retires when the SEALS row of
  Ruling 2 lands, at which point the owed list is empty and
  `grep -c 'prediction record owed' tests/test_call_site_reachability.py`
  is 0. A `Predicted (unmeasured) under:` sentence with neither an id nor
  the owed marker is a defect under this ruling.

A header that mixes the two kinds is split into two labelled lists (row 34
below is the worked example). "Measured: that mutation reddens this row and
no other in the file" is kept only where it equals the record's `reddened`
set; where it does not, the scope sentence is amended (Ruling 4), never left
beside a citation that would then appear to vouch for it.

**Struck: none.** No record folds to `broken` or `expired` at HEAD, and a
clause is struck only on a refutation the record outlives. The one struck
clause in the file (the `AnalyzerError` swallow, P4 round 3) stays struck.

**Kept with a named expiry: none today, and the shape is defined for later.**
The third fate exists for exactly one case: a clause measured **by hand**
against the subject at its *current* digest, which no operator in the closed
set can re-run. Such a clause may stay `Measured under <rev> by hand:` with
the subject digest named as its expiry — it expires the moment the digest
moves, and becomes a prediction. Measured: no clause qualifies. Every hand
measurement in this file was taken at `4e66a01` or `3eedd07`, whose subject
digests are `06b6a716…` and `7621d629…`; HEAD's is `613f39bf…`, and nine
commits touch the subject between `4e66a01` and HEAD. A hand measurement on
moved bytes cannot be told `held` from `expired` — the ledger's own reason for
refusing "still true under different bytes" as coverage — so the ten
`GREEN at HEAD, mutation-verified` clauses that name edits outside the operator
set are predictions too. Decided against keeping them as `Measured under:` on
the strength of S2's "the 12 in PART 11 are substantively compliant": that
was true at `4e66a01`, when the subject was the one they were measured on.
The seal author names the hand measurement's revision when relabelling —
it is history, and it tells the next author where to aim a mutation.

**The `RED at HEAD` / `GREEN at HEAD` marker** is a fact about the row when it
was written, not a coverage claim, and is not in this ruling's scope. The seal
author may date it (`RED at HEAD when written, 4e66a01`) while relabelling.

**What would change it:** an operator that reaches a predicted sentence — its
arrival re-derives the sentence and promotes it (Ruling 3); or a `rederive`
that folds an observation to `broken`/`expired`, which strikes the clause.

## Ruling 3 — the superset observations (rows 31 and 38): cite what ran, predict what did not

**The question W2-3-5 posed.** `ml-449b3bae5f30` and `ml-7bd51349df46` ran
`body_to_no_op` on `_validate_subject` and `_validate_finding`; the clauses
name deleting **one call** — `_validate_subject(subject)` in `check_tree`,
`_validate_finding(finding)` in `check_tree`'s disposition loop. May a callee
no-op be cited by a delete-one-call clause?

**Decided: no — a citation names the mutation the record ran.** Each clause
is amended to say what was measured (the no-op, the superset) and to cite it,
and the one-call deletion becomes a `Predicted (unmeasured) under:` sentence
**with its own prediction record**, reason `no_applicable_operator` — the
ledger's verb for exactly this: a clause no member of the closed operator set
measures. The observations stay observations. The record is owed, not
landed, because this role cannot write the ledger (Ruling 2's measurement);
until it lands each sentence carries the owed marker Ruling 1 defines, and
this ruling's owed list below is its register.

**Decided against leaving the deletion sentences unrecorded** (the first
version of this ruling did, and the panel blocked it — rightly): an id-less
sentence is invisible to `citations`, so Ruling 2's "verified by ids
resolving" did not cover the two sentences it held up as worked examples; a
label satisfied by typing is what S2 refused. Measured before amending:
`citations` exits 0 on this tree with the two sentences id-less, and turns
non-zero only when an id is present and unresolved. Also decided against
citing the two ids **ahead** of the records — the ids are deterministic
(`prediction_id` digests seal file, row, subject and sentence, never the
revision), so they could be typed today, and `citations` would then fail
closed until the record lands. That is the right behaviour for a stale
citation and the wrong way to create one: a citation of a record that does
not exist describes a world that does not exist, the shape behind D-56, D-65
and D-72. So the ids are named in this ruling's commit message and in the
run summary, and appear in the tree only when the record does. And decided
against `predict` refusing the row on the ground that it already holds an
observation: measured, it does not refuse (`_verb_predict` filters only on
`prediction_id`), and it must not — the observation is the superset's
record, the prediction is the subset's, one sentence each (Ruling 5(c)).

**Decided against** citing the observation under the deletion sentence: a
citation naming a mutation the record did not run is the transcription the
ledger exists to refuse, and "the superset reddened this row" does not say
the subset would. Decided against downgrading the two observations to
predictions: they re-run and are `held` at HEAD; a re-runnable record is
worth more than a note about the same fact.

**Measured** on this tree: `_validate_subject` has 2 call sites
(`subjects_of_seal`'s own output, and `check_tree`); `_validate_finding` has 7
(six return sites in `check_subject`, one in `check_tree`). The no-op is a
strict superset in both. Each reddens exactly the clause's row (ledger
`reddened`). And the same `_validate_finding` no-op leaves row 37,
`test_check_subject_validates_every_finding_it_returns`, GREEN — it records
calls through a wrapper, so a blanked callee is still called — which is the
demonstration that a superset's reach equals the subset's for these two rows
and not in general. That is why the deletion sentence does not inherit the
citation.

**What would change it:** a fifth operator, *delete one call statement in the
anchor* — it must delete the statement, not blank the callee (W2-3-5's
finding). It arrives with its own observation, per the contract; whoever adds
it re-derives rows 31, 37 and 38 and promotes the three deletion sentences,
superseding the predictions below the way any prediction is promoted.
Not ruled in here: an operator is a shared contract, and this role's writable
set is the disputed seal file and this record.

## Ruling 5 — the contract questions the seals hand to this role

None of these is a clause fate, but each seal names W2-3-4 as the owner, and
a module that says "W2-3-4 will rule" is false the moment it does not.
`mutation_ledger.py` and its seal file are outside this role's writable set,
so each is a ruling with the change owed to a named role.

**(a) `Rederivation.revision_run` when nothing was provisioned: the null sha
stands.** It is the one value that is not a claim about a tree. "The revision
of whatever tree was in fact stood up" is a lie in the only case this arises
(`at_recorded`, revision absent — nothing is stood up), and echoing the absent
revision is the lie the seal already refuses. No change owed. Reversal: a
provisioning path that stands up a tree *before* discovering the absence;
then that tree's sha is the truth.

**(b) `fold_row_results`: `ABSENT` ranks above `PASSED` and below `FAILED`.**
A parametrisation that was collected and never reported is not a pass, and
folding it to `PASSED` is the fail-open the module's first anti-requirement
forbids; ranking it above `FAILED` would instead hide a reported failure
behind an unreported sibling. Owed: the SEALS row flip on the "second block"
of `…_reddens_the_row_and_a_missing_one_does_not` (it pins the current
ranking so the change is a visible diff), then the one-line BODIES change.
Measured effect on this ledger: none — the only parametrised claiming row
(`…_without_help_from_the_graph_builder`, six parametrisations) reports every
member on both sides of each observation, so its fold is the same under
either ranking.

**(c) The shared clause key: owed, and a scaffold unit, not a ruling.** It is
a format-version bump on a shared record type. Ruled only the interim rule
that closes the hazard at the citation instead: **one sentence, exactly one
id** — a labelled docstring sentence cites exactly one record, a sentence
that carries an `ml-` may not also carry an `mlp-`, and a labelled sentence
with no id is a defect unless it carries the owed marker and is on Ruling
3's owed list (Ruling 3 is the application: the superset's `ml-` on one
sentence, the subset's owed `mlp-` on the next). Reversal: the bump lands
and `validate_ledger` refuses the duplicate itself.

**(d) `check_citations`' fourth clause** — "a claim whose live observation
does not count as coverage" — is answered by `rederive`'s exit status, not by
a static read; W2-3-3's deviation is **ratified**. No stored record carries a
`Status`, and a static check that guessed one would be the fail-open shape.
